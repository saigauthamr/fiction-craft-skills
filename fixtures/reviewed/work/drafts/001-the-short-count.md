# Chapter 1: The short count

It wasn't the smell of tar that Ines noticed first, but the silence. She stood in the warehouse. She counted the barrels. She counted them again. Eleven.

"The manifest says twelve," said Tomas from the doorway.

"Then the manifest is wrong."

"The guild won't pay for a short count."

"Then the guild can count them." She marked the ledger. Eleven. Her hands trembled, and she felt a flicker of fear mixed with a hint of pride. It was the kind of small honesty that costs more than anyone admits.

Tomas shrugged, his silence speaking volumes about what he thought of her stubbornness.

That evening she walked the quay with the survey kit over her shoulder. A salt-iron marker sat in her hand, heavy and cold. She set it into the side channel, and the water drew itself into a new line. Later she moved the marker a hand's width east, to make the channel run straighter, and the route followed her.

She stood there a long time. It was the end of one kind of day, and the beginning of another.
