# Learning from your edits

When the AI drafts a chapter and you edit it by hand, `story-edits` compares your version with the exact text it handed over, sorts your changes by what they did, asks about the ones it cannot explain, and saves the lessons you confirm to your voice file. This is one invented chapter, one run.

## What the writer changed

**Handed over**

> Ines set the marker at the edge of the channel just before the tide turned. She tightened the last bolt and stepped back. It was the moment she had waited for all season, and she felt a flicker of pride mixed with a hint of fear.

**After the writer's edit**

> Ines set the marker at the edge of the channel just before the tide turned. She tightened the last bolt and stepped back. Her hands were steady. She noticed that, and did not trust it.

Across the chapter the writer also cut the lines that explained a silence, a trembling voice, and the closing moment of realization, and shortened a speech to one line.

## The lessons it proposed

1. Do not state what a beat meant or what a character feels; show a small physical detail or action instead.
2. Keep speeches short, and do not let a character spell out their own opinion.

Each rests on several changes pointing the same way. A change that stood alone, with nothing else in the chapter behind it, was a question to the writer, never a lesson. A lesson the writer declined is recorded as `[rejected]` so it is not proposed again. Approved lessons go into the voice file as one unmarked line each, with no record of who approved what or when. The handed-over copy is deleted once the lessons are saved.
