# Dialogue, before and after

Both drafts come from the same beats (`evals/dialogue-pair/beats.md`): Ines confronts her uncle Teo, the guild's tally-master, with proof that the ferry counts have been inflated for years. One draft was written with `story-draft` before the dialogue guidance existed; the other with it. The full drafts are in `evals/dialogue-pair/`.

In a blind read, one reader preferred the second draft: more natural, lines of different sizes, none of the "it isn't X, it's Y" shape. One reader, one pair. The simple counts for the two drafts disagreed with that read in one place (see the folder's README), which is why they are guards and not verdicts.

## The opening exchange

**Before**

> "You're late," he said. "Supper was an hour ago."
>
> "I wasn't asked to supper."
>
> "You were always asked. You just never came." He turned a page. "What have you got there?"

**After**

> "You're late for supper," Teo said.
>
> "I wasn't invited."
>
> "You're always invited." He tore the bread in half and held out the bigger piece. She did not take it. He put it down on the blotter, on top of a column of figures, and she watched the grease spread into the paper.

The first version trades neat lines, each answering the last, and ends by handing the scene its question. In the second, the uncle's offer of bread carries the family history, and Ines's refusal of it is the answer.

## The argument itself

**Before**

> "Tickets get lost."
>
> "Lost tickets make the ledger lower than the count. Not higher."

**After**

> "You were on the landing for the whole of spring."
>
> "Two thousand six hundred and some."
>
> "Landings aren't the ferry."

In the first, Ines states the logic of her own proof, which is there for the reader. In the second, Teo makes a specific objection (boats also cross at other landings), so the argument moves by new facts instead of restating the point.

## What changed in the guidance

- An action between lines has to do something: change who holds the scene, or show what a character will not say. Decorative motion on every line is a different problem.
- A cornered character dodges, deflects, or objects on a detail. They do not brief the reader.
- Lines of different length, and some that do not answer the last one.
- The narration leaves the meaning of the scene to the reader.
