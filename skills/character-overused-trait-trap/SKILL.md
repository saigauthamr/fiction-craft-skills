# The Overused Trait Trap

A relatable trait is a strength until it becomes the only thing the character is. Then readers stop being charmed and begin to be irritated. Think of a character whose single relatable motivation, say worry about his child, is invoked in every scene until the audience dreads his appearances. A comic exaggeration: a character who responds to every situation with a lunch order.

## How it happens

1. A trait works in an early scene, so the writer reuses it.
2. The trait becomes the character's answer to everything.
3. Other wants, skills, and contradictions never appear, so there is nothing else to enjoy.
4. The reader starts anticipating the trait and resenting it.

## Checks

- `character.trait.01` List the traits the reader has seen for each recurring character. How many scenes in a row has one trait been the driver?
- `character.trait.02` What else does this character want, and has it ever competed with the trait?
- `character.trait.03` Could the trait be absent from a scene without breaking the character? If not, the trait is carrying too much.

## Fixes

- **Give competing wants.** A parent who is also ambitious and sometimes resents the child is a person, not a slogan. Wants that conflict create choices.
- **Rotate the spotlight.** Let a different trait or skill carry some scenes. Rest the main one so it lands when it returns.
- **Let the trait cost something and change.** An unresolved flaw that blocks the plot too long becomes a liability. See `character-flaws-restrictions-limitations`.
- **Move quirks from decoration to function.** Quirks work best when they expand or break the character's role, and they grate when they are random. A hardened soldier who restores clocks tells us about patience. A character who is just loud and strange tells us nothing.
- **Vary the domain.** Check which area of the character's life each scene touches. See the sub-areas idea in `character-proactive-relatable-capable`.

## Archetypes

Archetypes help readers sort a character quickly, and that is the right starting point. The trap is staying in the box. Once the reader has sorted the character, widen the box over time or subvert it with a reversal.

## Cautions and exceptions

- Repetition is useful for a comic side character who appears briefly. The danger rises with screen time and with viewpoint characters.
- Use the same check on strengths. A trademark skill used in every scene turns into a one-note capability. See `character-capability-design`.
- Relatability traits in `character-relatability-toolkit` should be varied and shown by action, not repeated.
- Giving a character a distinct first impression is a separate task. See `character-presenting-a-character`.
