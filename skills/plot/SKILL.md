# Plot

Plot has two jobs: it gives conflict something to happen in, and it gives the reader a sense that the story is going somewhere. This hub routes to the framework skills that make both concrete. Load only the child you need.

## Children

**The core model**
- `plot-promise-progress-payoff`: the core model. Load first for any plot diagnosis or plan.
- `plot-kinds-of-promises`: what an opening promises (tone, plot, character, series) and how to check it.
- `plot-types-of-progress`: how to measure and signpost progress, and match it to the climax.
- `plot-cold-open-microcosm`: opening with a small version of the whole story.
- `plot-big-p-little-p`: the book-level Plot versus chapter-level plot.

**Structure and shape**
- `plot-story-circle`: an eight-step map for transformation stories, and how to bend it.
- `plot-three-act-structure`: the beats of three acts and the question that drives them.
- `plot-structure-model`: studying a narrow pattern across many works to build your own version.
- `plot-archetypes`: building a plot from layered archetypes, and making an arc go wrong on purpose.
- `plot-scale-of-plot`: matching scope to length, from short story to novel.

**Scene and chapter momentum**
- `plot-try-fail-cycle`: attempts that fail in ways that matter, so victories are earned.
- `plot-yes-but-no-and`: chaining escalating complications when you are stuck on what happens next.
- `plot-layering-conflict`: carrying inner and outer conflict together, and signposting an arc.

**Climax, twists, and payoff**
- `plot-twists-promise-and-wish`: promise one thing, make the reader wish for another, deliver that.
- `plot-payoff-deniability-forgotten-promise`: surprising payoffs without a twist.
- `plot-cheer-moment-sanderlanche`: the moment readers cheer, and staggering a cascade of payoffs.
- `plot-climax-placement-aftermath`: where the biggest scene goes, and how long the aftermath runs.
- `plot-foreshadowing-twists`: how many readers should see a twist coming, and how to hide a clue.

**Planning**
- `plot-outline-interwoven-cycles`: outlining several plotlines, for outline-first and discovery writers alike.

## Notes

- The model is mainly an analysis tool. Writers can use it while discovery writing, and outliners can use it to plan; neither has to master it before drafting.
