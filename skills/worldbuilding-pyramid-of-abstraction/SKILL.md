# Pyramid of Abstraction

Words sit on a pyramid. At the bottom are concrete images readers all picture the same way. At the top are abstractions such as love, destiny, or the nature of a magic system. Each descriptive word pulls a concept down toward the concrete. Even "a dog" is fairly abstract, because every reader pictures a different dog. "A small, wet poodle huddled in an alley, whimpering" puts everyone in the same place.

The practical rule is that readers will follow you up the pyramid into explanation, philosophy, or a character's navel-gazing only if you have first anchored them in a concrete scene.

## Using it in a scene

1. **Anchor first.** Open with concrete sensory detail. Use more than sight: smell, texture, temperature, sound, taste.
2. **Climb while staying anchored.** Move into the character's thoughts, the history of a place, or how the magic works, but keep the reader in the room while you do it.
3. **Re-anchor.** Drop back to a sensory detail or a physical action every so often. How often is a judgment call. If a reader could not say where the characters are standing, you waited too long.
4. **Close concretely.** A useful pattern for dialogue is an anchor at the start, a few beats in the middle, and an anchor at the end. Too many beats clutter the talk.

## Example

A scene where two characters argue about a prophecy. Unanchored, it is two voices exchanging lore. Anchored, one of them has sand in her teeth and the spice shimmers in the lamplight while they talk, so the prophecy is discussed on a specific planet at a specific hour.

## Checks

- `worldbuilding.pyramid.01` Find the most abstract paragraph (an explanation, a reflection, a cosmology). What concrete detail sits in the paragraphs just before and after it?
- `worldbuilding.pyramid.02` Does the scene use only sight? Add one or two other senses.
- `worldbuilding.pyramid.03` Is any concrete detail doing no work? When the image is irrelevant, a plain noun is enough.
- `worldbuilding.pyramid.04` Is a figure of speech colliding with the world, such as "he flew across the room" where people really fly?
- `worldbuilding.pyramid.05` Does a prologue about gods or cosmology give the reader no character and no scene?

## Cautions and exceptions

- This is a tool for managing attention, not a quota of adjectives. With practice anchoring becomes automatic, and a writing group can flag the places where they lost the scene or the viewpoint.
- Pair it with `worldbuilding-viewpoint-filtered-description` so the anchors reflect what this character would notice, and with `worldbuilding-hollow-iceberg` to decide how much of the hidden world to surface at all.
