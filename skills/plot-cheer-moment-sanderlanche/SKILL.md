# The Cheer Moment and the Sanderlanche

A stand-up-and-cheer moment is a payoff the reader has been waiting for. It lands because something promised finally happens. A Sanderlanche is a run of such payoffs near the end of a book, each one triggering the next.

## Building the cheer

1. **Promise it early.** Show readers what the triumph could look like, such as a weak hero who will become strong. Readers will then tolerate the character being beaten down on the way.
2. **Punish before paying.** Sustained setbacks make success feel due. A heavy cost can earn a cheer even with a modest arc.
3. **Land the internal payoff first.** The character makes the emotional decision, such as trusting a friend or an ability, and that choice carries them into success on the external problem. A returning ally and a hero who trusts their instinct can both decide, and then win because both decided.
4. **Then the external payoff.** The physical victory follows the choice.

Internal first, external second is a rule of thumb. It can be reversed when the story reads better that way.

## Merging several arcs

When overlapping arcs all converge on one moment, check that each arc has its own distinct beat in the sequence. You may not get the convergence right in the first draft, or the fifth.

## Staggering the cascade

- Too many awesome events at once produce numbness. Space the payoffs in an order that builds.
- Rank them. Each should be larger than or different from the last.
- Give a minor character their climax earlier, around the two-thirds mark, so they can step aside and not compete with the main character at the end.
- In revision, cut payoffs and twists if too many pile up. A cascade can overreach.

## Checks

- `plot.cheer.01` Name the promise this cheer moment pays off, and where it was made.
- `plot.cheer.02` What internal decision comes before the external win? The reverse order is fine when it reads better.
- `plot.cheer.03` If several plotlines resolve in one scene, does each get its own distinct beat?
- `plot.cheer.04` Is each payoff in a cascade larger than or different from the last, and not only louder?

## Cautions and exceptions

- A late cascade works best when the earlier chapters built the arcs it resolves. For making promises and signposting progress, see `plot-promise-progress-payoff` and `plot-types-of-progress`.
- If the cascade surprises, see `plot-payoff-deniability-forgotten-promise`. For where the biggest moment sits in the book, see `plot-climax-placement-aftermath`.
