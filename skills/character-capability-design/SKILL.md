# Capability Design

Readers enjoy watching competent people do things competently. A heist crew, a master detective, and a veteran spy all draw on that pleasure. Capability is a scale, and the right setting depends on the story.

## Design rules

1. **Choose one or two areas of excellence.** Make the character truly excellent there and weak in others. A character good at everything has no texture and no need for allies.
2. **Let early strengths advance the plot.** Show the skill solving a problem in the first act, so the reader trusts it.
3. **Exploit the weaknesses.** Build scenes in which what the character lacks decides what happens. A brilliant strategist who panics in a crowd forces the plot into crowds.
4. **Let capability grow when the arc calls for it.** Apprentice stories move the character up this scale as the main arc.

## Capability beyond combat

A skill is anything that matters to the story's problem. A loyal gardener whose steadiness saves the quest, or a medic whose patience holds a team together, carries as much weight as a swordsman. If your story is about loyalty, loyalty can be the character's superpower and the thing that wins the ending.

## The cost of too much

High capability tends to lower relatability, since readers cannot see themselves in someone who never struggles. Two ways to handle it.

- Pair the strength with vulnerability, a relatable want, or a visible limit. A spy who can do anything but must keep a promise to a dying friend is easier to follow.
- Write a story that is about being proactive and superb. Readers of such stories are there for the competence, and they will accept a high setting.

## The overpowered or divine figure

What makes a nearly invincible character interesting is what they cannot do. Choose a restriction or limitation that matters to the story's central problem, and give them a relatable want. See `character-flaws-restrictions-limitations`. Consider using such a figure sparingly as viewpoint, as discussed in `character-relatability-toolkit`.

## Checks

- `character.capability.01` Name the one or two things this character does better than anyone else in the story. Where does the plot use them?
- `character.capability.02` Name two things they do badly. Where does the plot punish that?
- `character.capability.03` If the character fails nothing, where would a reader worry?

## Cautions and exceptions

- Capability is one of three scales. A character can be capable and passive or capable and unlikeable. See `character-proactive-relatable-capable`.
- A strength repeated in every scene can turn into a single-note trait. See `character-overused-trait-trap`.
- Villains are often highly capable. See `character-villain-and-antihero-patterns`.
- Magic abilities have their own design rules, covered in the magic worldbuilding skills.
