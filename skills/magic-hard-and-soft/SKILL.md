# Hard and Soft Magic

Magic sits on a spectrum. At the hard end, readers can learn the rules and predict what the magic will do, and characters win by mastering and exploiting those rules, much as scientists or engineers would. At the soft end, readers do not know the rules and cannot guess the outcome, so the magic produces wonder or dread. Neither end is better. They do different jobs.

## Place your system

1. **Ask what the reader can anticipate.** Describe a scene where the magic is used. Could a reader guess what it will do next, and what it cannot do? If yes, that part is hard. If the honest answer is "anything", that part is soft.
2. **Place each element separately.** One story can hold both. A ring whose effects are clearly stated (invisibility, being seen by a villain, corruption) is hard, while the wizard beside the ring hobbit is soft because nobody on the page knows his limits.
3. **Check across the whole work.** A series can be hard inside each book and soft across the series if later books forget the consequences of earlier inventions. That gives whimsy and costs consistency. Choose it on purpose.
4. **Pick the job you want.** Hard magic suits stories about clever problem-solving, heists, and characters who train. Soft magic suits horror, wonder, and urban fantasy where the protagonist should lack control. Hybrids exist: a wish-granting object where the reader knows wishes come but not their price leans soft.

## Moving along the spectrum

- A story can start soft and become hard as characters work out the rules.
- A story can present rules and later reveal they were wrong. This is harder to pull off and works best when the whole plot is built around it.
- What the author knows can differ from what the text shows. Knowing the rules of a mysterious power helps you keep it consistent even if the reader never sees them.

## Checks

- `magic.hardsoft.01` For each magical element, could a reader predict what it will do and what it cannot do? Place each element separately.
- `magic.hardsoft.02` Does each element's place on the spectrum fit its job: problem-solving and training for hard magic, wonder and lack of control for soft?
- `magic.hardsoft.03` Across a series, are earlier inventions' consequences remembered, or is the drift toward softness chosen on purpose?

## Cautions and exceptions

- Hardness comes from what the reader can predict, which is separate from how scientific the system sounds. See `magic-internal-and-external-logic`.
- The place on the spectrum limits how you can resolve conflicts. See `magic-first-law`.
- Stories where nature is the magic, such as a recurring deadly phenomenon, tend to lean hard, because characters race to understand and stop it.
- If you need a hint that the rules might be wrong, flag it early: state how the magic is believed to work, add a few historical cases nobody can explain, and let a mentor deflect certain questions.
