# Alpha and Beta Readers

Two kinds of early reader do different jobs on a finished draft.

## The two roles

- **Alpha readers** are a few trusted people with deep knowledge of writing and a long relationship with you. They may tell you what to change, and you ask for their judgment.
- **Beta readers** are a test audience drawn from the people likely to enjoy this kind of book. They report how the book felt and where it dragged, confused, or excited them. They do not tell you how to fix it.

## Using them

1. Give the draft only when you cannot see how another pass alone would improve it. Another draft that only shuffles things sideways is the signal to ask for readers.
2. Ask betas for reactions. Where did you want to stop reading? What did you expect to happen? Who did you like? Mark the page where each reaction occurred.
3. Look for patterns across readers. Three people stalling in the same chapter is data. One person's complaint is a data point.
4. Find the cause yourself. A reader may say the book is slow and ask for an action scene. The cause could instead be that a promise was dropped or a tangent runs too long. Treat the reader as a reliable alarm and an unreliable mechanic.
5. Feed what you decide to keep into a revision guide. See `foundations-revision-guide`.

## Checks

- `foundations.readers.01` Across readers, which reactions form a pattern (several readers stalling or lost in the same place), and which are single data points?
- `foundations.readers.02` For each pattern, what is the cause in the story, as opposed to the fix a reader suggested?

## Cautions and exceptions

- Do not weigh any single beta reader too heavily.
- Not every writer likes beta readers. Some rely on a few trusted alpha readers alone, and learning revision after only one book is hard, so lean on those trusted people while your instinct builds.
- Keep your own process. If readers make a draft worse each time, change who you ask or when.
- Discovery writers should get feedback only on finished drafts. See `foundations-gardener-architect` and `foundations-writing-group-rules` for how to receive notes without defending.
