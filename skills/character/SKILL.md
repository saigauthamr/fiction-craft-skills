# Character

Characters are read through what they want, how much they do about it, and how easy they are to care about. This hub routes to the skills that make those things concrete. Load only the child you need.

## Children

**The three scales and what moves them**
- `character-proactive-relatable-capable`: the three sliding scales, and describing an arc as movement along them. Load first for a flat or wooden character.
- `character-proactivity-toolkit`: a passive, reluctant, restricted, depressed, or stalled character, or a villain who outshines the hero.
- `character-relatability-toolkit`: techniques for making a character easy to care about.
- `character-capability-design`: choosing what a character is good at and bad at so strengths and weaknesses drive the plot.

**Weaknesses, villains, and traps**
- `character-flaws-restrictions-limitations`: sorting a character's weaknesses into flaws, restrictions, and limitations.
- `character-villain-and-antihero-patterns`: where villains and antiheroes sit on the scales, and a villain's fall.
- `character-overused-trait-trap`: a quirk or relatable trait repeated until it grates.

**Presenting and understanding a character**
- `character-iconic-characters`: series leads and detectives who do not change, and building on competence and cost.
- `character-first-entry`: introducing an important character so readers can place them: role, link to the scene, first impression, one trait.
- `character-presenting-a-character`: showing motivation, personality, and values without announcing them.
- `character-motivation-discovery-questions`: a question set to run on your own character when you do not know why they act.
- `character-writing-unfamiliar-backgrounds`: writing a character whose experience you have not lived.

**Change and scenes**
- `character-arc-types`: the shapes a character arc can take, and how to plan one.
- `character-floating-outline-for-dialogue`: planning a dialogue scene around what each speaker wants.

## Which child for which problem

| The writer says | Load |
| --- | --- |
| "Readers can't picture her" / "the villain walks in and nobody knows who he is" / "I introduced them but it doesn't land" | `character-first-entry` |
| "My character is boring" / "readers don't care about him" / "she feels wooden" | `character-proactive-relatable-capable`, then `character-proactivity-toolkit` or `character-relatability-toolkit` |
| "My hero is too perfect" / "too helpless" / "what should she be good at?" | `character-capability-design` |
| "What weakness should I give him?" | `character-flaws-restrictions-limitations` |
| "My villain is flat" / "the villain is more interesting than the hero" / "I'm writing an antihero" | `character-villain-and-antihero-patterns` |
| "One quirk keeps showing up and it grates" | `character-overused-trait-trap` |
| "My detective never changes" / "series lead has no arc" | `character-iconic-characters` |
| "Everyone sounds the same" / "how do I show who she is?" | `character-presenting-a-character` |
| "I don't know why he does this" | `character-motivation-discovery-questions` |
| "I'm writing someone from a background I haven't lived" | `character-writing-unfamiliar-backgrounds` |
| "How should my character change?" / "the arc feels flat" | `character-arc-types`, `plot-layering-conflict` |
| "My dialogue scene is flat" | `character-floating-outline-for-dialogue` |

## Notes

- A character's arc and the plot's progress are linked. For how an arc is signposted through the middle of a story, see `plot-layering-conflict`.
- For prose that explains a character's feelings instead of showing them, see `prose-ai-tells`.
