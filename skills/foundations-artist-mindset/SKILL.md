# The Artist Mindset

There are two roles in a writing life, and they should not trade places. While you draft, you are the artist, and the artist decides what is best for the story and its characters. Questions about what might be popular belong to a later, separate stage and should not steer the drafting. This skill covers only the artist's side, the part that shapes the pages.

## Keep the artist in charge of the draft

1. **Decide by story.** At each choice, ask what this book and these characters need. Do not ask what a hypothetical audience would like.
2. **Write the book you want to write.** Chasing a trend means writing toward a moving target, and the market will have moved by the time you finish. Your enthusiasm shows on the page.
3. **Do not compromise the work in revision to chase approval.** When you revise, fix what is wrong for the story. A change made only to look more marketable usually weakens the book.
4. **Let the two roles talk without letting one dictate.** It is fine to notice what kind of stories excite you or what is missing in the books you read, and then let the artist write something in that space. The outside view informs the choice of project and never overrules the content of the draft.

## Early books are practice

- The product of your writing time is you. Each book makes you more skilled and more empathetic, whatever happens to the manuscript.
- Treat the first several novels as scales on a piano. Your job is to write enough to learn your own process.
- If your skill falls short of your vision and the draft looks ugly, keep going. The gap is normal, and finishing teaches what stopping cannot.
- Weak points you force yourself to learn, such as revision, can become strengths.

## Checks

None. This skill is about the writer's process and mindset, not a check to run on a story.

## Cautions and exceptions

- The split between artist and outside concerns is deliberately exaggerated to make the point. Real writers blend them, and for some the advice is wrong.
- Do not read this as permission to ignore readers. Feedback on a finished draft is valuable. See `foundations-alpha-beta-readers`. The point is the order: draft for the story, then listen.
- Ideas are cheap and execution is what counts. Readers and editors can tell years of practice, so protect time for practice. See `foundations-chef-not-cook`.
