# Big P Plot and little p plot

"Plot" means two things, and many problems come from mixing them up.

- **Big P Plot** is what the book is about, stated in a sentence or two.
- **little p plot** is what happens chapter by chapter: the small problems raised and resolved that hold the reader and build toward larger ones.

## Examples

- In one epic quest, the Big P Plot is destroying the ring and the enemy's power. A little p plot beat is disguising yourself to slip past guards.
- In one ensemble adventure, the Big P Plot is chasing a McGuffin to save the world. The little p plot that the film actually runs on is whether these people can become friends.

## Using the distinction

- Write the Big P Plot as one or two sentences. If you cannot, the book is not yet focused.
- Check that each chapter's little p plot advances a promise of the Big P Plot, or makes a promise of its own that the book keeps. See `plot-promise-progress-payoff`.
- The little p plot can give a tone promise or act as a microcosm of the larger story, so the Big P Plot can arrive at the end of act one without the opening feeling aimless. See `plot-cold-open-microcosm`.
- The soul of a story is often in the little p plot, not the headline plot. See `plot-types-of-progress`.

## Checks

- `plot.bigp.01` State the Big P Plot in one or two sentences. If you cannot, the book is not focused yet.
- `plot.bigp.02` For each chapter, name its little p plot and whether it advances a Big P promise or makes a promise of its own that the book keeps.
- `plot.bigp.03` Where does the story's emotional core actually live, in the headline plot or in a little p thread, and do the signposts follow it?
