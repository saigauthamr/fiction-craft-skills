# Evals: foundations hub

Each entry is a realistic writer prompt, the skill expected to trigger, and what a good answer must show. Run each prompt with the skills installed and without, and compare.

---

## found-01 - Rules that do not fit
**Prompt:** My writing teacher says every chapter must end on a cliffhanger. I did that in my quiet literary fantasy and it feels cheap, but I don't want to break the rule. What do I do?
**Should trigger:** `foundations-chef-not-cook`
**Must show:**
- Asks what effect the rule is meant to produce for the reader, instead of obeying or dismissing it
- Frames the writer as a chef who diagnoses which ingredient is off and finds another way to get the effect
- Notes that some advice will be wrong for this writer, and suggests testing it on the pages
**Must not rely on:** generic advice like "rules are made to be broken" or "trust your gut"

## found-02 - Outline or not
**Prompt:** I outlined my whole thriller, chapter by chapter, and now I have no desire to write it. My friend says real writers just start typing. Which one am I supposed to be?
**Should trigger:** `foundations-gardener-architect`
**Must show:**
- Describes the architect-to-gardener spectrum and says most writers blend
- Recognises that losing enthusiasm after outlining is a known discovery-writer pattern
- Suggests trying both methods and building a blended, changing process (for example outline only the ending and key turns)
- Names the risk at each end, including over-revising early chapters for gardeners
**Must not rely on:** "do whatever works for you" with no spectrum or risks

## found-03 - Writing to please
**Prompt:** Vampire stories are everywhere, so I'm turning my ghost novel into a vampire novel. I'm halfway through and it feels wrong, but readers will like it more. Also my first draft looks awful next to what I imagined.
**Should trigger:** `foundations-artist-mindset`
**Must show:**
- Says story decisions during drafting belong to the writer-as-artist, not to a guess about what readers want
- Warns that chasing a trend aims at a moving target and drains enthusiasm
- Treats the gap between vision and ugly draft as normal and says to keep finishing
- Frames early books as practice that improves the writer
**Must not rely on:** discussion of publishing, agents, or markets

## found-04 - The critique group
**Prompt:** I'm a pantser and posted my first six chapters to my critique group. Now four people want four different things and I keep defending my choices in the comments. I've stopped writing. How do I fix this?
**Should trigger:** `foundations-writing-group-rules`
**Must show:**
- Tells a discovery writer to hold advice until the draft is done and another book is under way
- Says to stay quiet and write down feedback instead of defending
- Advises that givers be descriptive, not prescriptive, reporting reactions and confusion instead of fixes
- Says to expect to use only a fraction of the notes
**Must not rely on:** "take feedback with a grain of salt"

## found-05 - Overwhelmed by notes
**Prompt:** I got a page of feedback on my finished novel, from the villain being dull to typos to a slow middle to a confusing magic system. I don't know where to start and I keep fixing commas.
**Should trigger:** `foundations-revision-guide`
**Must show:**
- Turns the notes into a ranked list with the most important problems first
- Makes a specific plan for the top item, including where to add scenes or foreshadowing
- Limits the pass to one or two goals and ticks items off
**Must not rely on:** "revise in several passes" with no ranking or specific plan

## found-06 - One reader says add action
**Prompt:** One of my test readers said my book is slow and I need a fight scene in the middle. Two others said nothing about that but liked the ending. I'm ready to add the fight. Should I?
**Should trigger:** `foundations-alpha-beta-readers`
**Must show:**
- Says readers are good at noticing something is wrong and poor at diagnosing why
- Advises not weighing a single beta reader heavily and looking for patterns across readers
- Suggests looking for the real cause, such as a dropped promise or a tangent, before adding a scene
- Distinguishes alpha readers who may advise from betas who report reactions
**Must not rely on:** "get more feedback" without the diagnosis idea
