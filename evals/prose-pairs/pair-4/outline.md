# Chapter 1: Tuesday

**Goal:** show Noor competent with death at work and helpless with it at home, and plant the second phone.
**Viewpoint and tense:** Noor, first person, past tense.
**Promises carried:** tone (dry, funny in the wrong places); plot (what her father hid); character (she keeps score).

## Scenes
1. Just before seven in the morning, straight off a night shift, Noor lets herself into her father's house, four days after the funeral, to feed Biscuit and empty the fridge. She wants to do the chores and leave without feeling anything. It turns out the freezer is full of the meals she batch-cooked for him, labelled in her handwriting, months of them, uneaten, and the bin and the fridge are full of takeaway boxes from Beit Sitti, in Ferrow.
2. Looking for bin bags, she finds a cheap second phone in the kitchen drawer, charged. She wants not to look. She looks. It turns out there is one contact, "S", and a call every Tuesday evening for three years, at the hour her shift started.

**Turn (a note for the writer, not a line for the page):** the one who stayed was the one he had to hide things from.
**Hook out:** the phone rings in her hand. It is Tuesday.
**Length:** 1,000 to 1,400 words.

Background: Noor is a hospice night nurse who looked after her father for six years. Her brother Sami left nine years ago and runs the kitchen at Beit Sitti.
