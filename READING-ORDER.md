# Reading order

Every skill in the order that builds understanding, with a link to each file. 82 skills, about 56,741 words, roughly 4 hours 7 minutes at reading pace.

**To read everything in one file**, open `reading-edition.md`. It has a linked table of contents and every skill in this order.

**A hub** is a short index that routes to its children, so read the hub first and then its children in the listed order.

## Part 1. Setting up and moving through a story

Read these first. Onboarding decides who plans, drafts, and revises; the rest are the stages every story moves through, in order. Each stage adapts to those settings. (12 skills, about 84 minutes)

1. [`story-onboard`](skills/story-onboard/SKILL.md): Inspects existing material, offers presets, and shows the settings change for confirmation before writing. (4 min)
2. [`story-next`](skills/story-next/SKILL.md): Inspects project state and ownership, recommends one available next step, and stops without starting it. (2 min)
3. [`story-adopt`](skills/story-adopt/SKILL.md): Shows an import plan, preserves originals and provenance, resumes safely, and proposes facts for approval. (4 min)
4. [`story-develop`](skills/story-develop/SKILL.md): Asks questions or offers choices according to who leads, records settled decisions, and ends with a summary for confirmation. (6 min)
5. [`story-plan`](skills/story-plan/SKILL.md): Drafts plans when the AI owns planning and coaches when the writer does; records craft-check evidence and saves only after approval. (16 min)
6. [`story-draft`](skills/story-draft/SKILL.md): Follows drafting ownership and project voice; coaches when the writer drafts. (13 min)
7. [`story-check`](skills/story-check/SKILL.md): Cites evidence against the plan, craft checks, and machine-prose patterns. (20 min)
8. [`story-revise`](skills/story-revise/SKILL.md): The AI revises its own drafts with previous versions preserved and changes re-checked; when the writer owns revision, it gives notes and leaves prose unchanged. (6 min)
9. [`story-edits`](skills/story-edits/SKILL.md): Compares the delivered text with the edited chapter, groups the changes by what they did, asks about the ones it cannot explain, and saves approved lessons to the voice file. (4 min)
10. [`story-steer`](skills/story-steer/SKILL.md): Proposes numbered patches with their effects, applies approved changes, preserves old wording, and flags chapters needing revision. (3 min)
11. [`story-continue`](skills/story-continue/SKILL.md): Summarizes open promises, offers a next-chapter choice, then hands drafting to whoever owns it. (4 min)
12. [`story-learn`](skills/story-learn/SKILL.md): Teaches one technique with an example, exceptions, and a short exercise on the writer's material. (2 min)

## Part 2. Tools

Design tools any writer can use, whoever writes the prose: a rules lab for magic and world rules, character build sheets, and voice demos. (3 skills, about 13 minutes)

13. [`story-worldbuild`](skills/story-worldbuild/SKILL.md): Builds an ability table of costs, limits, and counters, hunts exploits, and tests what-ifs. (6 min)
14. [`story-cast`](skills/story-cast/SKILL.md): Builds sheets with wants, needs, three scales, weaknesses, voice, and arcs; gives the opposition its own plan. (4 min)
15. [`story-voice`](skills/story-voice/SKILL.md): Shows original demo passages from the writer's story, supports choices and blends, and saves the chosen voice only after approval. (2 min)

## Part 3. Process and mindset

How a writer works, takes feedback, and revises. (7 skills, about 12 minutes)

16. [`foundations`](skills/foundations/SKILL.md) (hub): Routes to process and mindset frameworks. (1 min)
17. [`foundations-chef-not-cook`](skills/foundations-chef-not-cook/SKILL.md): Chef not cook is a way to learn tools by understanding what each one does, so you can diagnose problems and adapt advice to your own book. (2 min)
18. [`foundations-gardener-architect`](skills/foundations-gardener-architect/SKILL.md): Gardener versus architect (discovery writer versus outline writer) gives a spectrum, the typical risks at each end, and how to build a blended process. (2 min)
19. [`foundations-artist-mindset`](skills/foundations-artist-mindset/SKILL.md): The artist mindset keeps story decisions in the hands of the drafting writer, and treats early books as practice that improves the writer. (2 min)
20. [`foundations-writing-group-rules`](skills/foundations-writing-group-rules/SKILL.md): Writing-group rules protect a draft from early advice, set how to receive criticism, and keep feedback descriptive instead of prescriptive. (2 min)
21. [`foundations-revision-guide`](skills/foundations-revision-guide/SKILL.md): A revision guide turns feedback into a ranked, targeted list of fixes, like a programmer's bug list, so each pass has a goal. (2 min)
22. [`foundations-alpha-beta-readers`](skills/foundations-alpha-beta-readers/SKILL.md): Alpha and beta readers are two kinds of early reader with different jobs, and the skill explains how to weigh what each reports. (2 min)

## Part 4. Plot

The core model first, then structure, scene momentum, payoff, and planning. (22 skills, about 46 minutes)

23. [`plot`](skills/plot/SKILL.md) (hub): Routes to frameworks for promises, progress, conflict, twists, and climaxes. (3 min)
24. [`plot-promise-progress-payoff`](skills/plot-promise-progress-payoff/SKILL.md): Load to diagnose why a story, chapter, or sequence feels boring or pointless, to check whether an ending satisfies, or to plan a plot around what the reader is led to expect. (2 min)
25. [`plot-kinds-of-promises`](skills/plot-kinds-of-promises/SKILL.md): Load when planning or revising a story's opening, a prologue, a first chapter, or a chapter opener, or when readers say the book was not what they expected. (2 min)
26. [`plot-types-of-progress`](skills/plot-types-of-progress/SKILL.md): Load when a middle drags, a sequence feels pointless, a plot has several threads, or a climax does not feel earned. (2 min)
27. [`plot-cold-open-microcosm`](skills/plot-cold-open-microcosm/SKILL.md): Load when a writer is designing an opening scene or prologue, wants a hook that previews the book's shape, or asks how to make an opening promise the main conflict. (1 min)
28. [`plot-big-p-little-p`](skills/plot-big-p-little-p/SKILL.md): Load when a writer cannot say what their book is about, when chapters feel disconnected, or when the small-scale action and the main story pull in different directions. (1 min)
29. [`plot-story-circle`](skills/plot-story-circle/SKILL.md): Gives the eight-step Story Circle, how to bend it into tragedy, and how to avoid turning it into a checklist. (3 min)
30. [`plot-three-act-structure`](skills/plot-three-act-structure/SKILL.md): Gives the beats of three-act structure, the major dramatic question, and how loosely to apply them in prose. (2 min)
31. [`plot-structure-model`](skills/plot-structure-model/SKILL.md): Gives the Structure Model method of studying a narrow pattern across many works and building your own version. (2 min)
32. [`plot-archetypes`](skills/plot-archetypes/SKILL.md): Gives a way to build a plot from layered archetypes and to make an arc go wrong on purpose. (3 min)
33. [`plot-scale-of-plot`](skills/plot-scale-of-plot/SKILL.md): Gives rules of thumb for matching scope to word count and reading experience. (2 min)
34. [`plot-try-fail-cycle`](skills/plot-try-fail-cycle/SKILL.md): Covers the Try-Fail Cycle and Points on the Map, plus when a run of successes can work instead. (2 min)
35. [`plot-yes-but-no-and`](skills/plot-yes-but-no-and/SKILL.md): Covers the Yes-but / No-and method for chaining escalating problems. (2 min)
36. [`plot-layering-conflict`](skills/plot-layering-conflict/SKILL.md): Covers layering internal and external conflict and signposting a character arc through the middle. (2 min)
37. [`plot-twists-promise-and-wish`](skills/plot-twists-promise-and-wish/SKILL.md): Gives the promise-and-wish method (promise one thing, make the reader wish for another, deliver that) and the alternatives of escalation and over-delivery. (2 min)
38. [`plot-payoff-deniability-forgotten-promise`](skills/plot-payoff-deniability-forgotten-promise/SKILL.md): Gives two ways to make a payoff surprising yet inevitable without a twist, plausible deniability and the forgotten promise. (2 min)
39. [`plot-cheer-moment-sanderlanche`](skills/plot-cheer-moment-sanderlanche/SKILL.md): Gives the stand-up-and-cheer moment, the order of internal then external payoff, and how to stagger a cascade of payoffs. (2 min)
40. [`plot-climax-placement-aftermath`](skills/plot-climax-placement-aftermath/SKILL.md): Gives three options for a strong early climax and notes on resolution length. (2 min)
41. [`plot-foreshadowing-twists`](skills/plot-foreshadowing-twists/SKILL.md): Gives a target for how many readers should guess, fixes for both extremes, and ways to hide a clue in plain sight. (2 min)
42. [`plot-outline-interwoven-cycles`](skills/plot-outline-interwoven-cycles/SKILL.md): Gives a method for listing each plot cycle's progress beats and merging them into parts, for outline-first and discovery writers. (2 min)
43. [`plot-causal-bridges`](skills/plot-causal-bridges/SKILL.md): Tests each major turn for why this person, why now, why this method, and what it changes. (2 min)
44. [`plot-reveal-boundaries`](skills/plot-reveal-boundaries/SKILL.md): Separates what is true, what each character knows, and when the reader learns it. (2 min)
